About Makar Sankranthi

Makar Sankranthi is one of the most important Hindu festivals celebrated across India, marking the transition of the sun into the zodiac sign of Capricorn (Makara). This auspicious day signifies the end of winter solstice and the beginning of longer days.

The festival is celebrated with great enthusiasm in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, where it spans over four days - Bhogi, Sankranthi, Kanuma, and Mukkanuma. Each day has its own significance and rituals.

Festival Traditions

🪁

Kite Flying

Colorful kites fill the sky as families and friends compete in kite flying competitions across rooftops.

🍯

Til-Gul Exchange

People exchange sesame seeds (til) and jaggery (gul) sweets while saying "Til-gul ghya, god god bola".

🔥

Bhogi Bonfire

Old and unwanted items are burned in a bonfire to symbolize new beginnings and prosperity.

🐄

Cattle Worship

Cows and bulls are decorated with colors, bells, and garlands as a mark of gratitude for their service.

🏺

Rangoli Art

Beautiful colorful patterns are drawn at the entrance of homes to welcome prosperity and good luck.

🎵

Folk Songs

Traditional folk songs and dances are performed to celebrate the harvest season and community spirit.
